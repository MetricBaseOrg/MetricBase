Weekly Brief — #28 · Week of November 9, 2026

Oil Falls 13% on Iran Peace Hopes, Bitcoin Slips to $75,760 After a Midterm Shock, and the JCI Ends Flat at 5,050 — What the Data Says

WTI settled at $90.83 and Brent at $98.32 after a weekly drop of about 13% on hopes of a US-Iran deal. Bitcoin lost 1.9% to $75,760 after a Thursday plunge as the Nasdaq fell 3.4% on the week. The JCI ended flat at 5,050 after a 4,904 close on Tuesday, and the rupiah firmed to 18,089 before the MSCI decision on November 11.

01 Energy Markets Energy

Last week we asked whether Brent would hold $117 or slip toward $113. It did neither. Brent settled at $98.32 on Friday on the January contract, which is now the front month after the December contract rolled off. WTI settled at $90.83, up 0.3% on the day. Reports put the weekly loss at about 13% for both grades, with estimates for Brent running from 12% to 16% depending on the contract used. By our arithmetic WTI fell 12.8% from $104.12.

The trigger was political. Reports described a Democratic sweep in the US midterms, read by traders as a rebuke of the Iran war and a brake on further military action. On Wednesday, December WTI fell $7.97 to $88.12 and January Brent $8.98 to $94.62, two-month lows. Prices bounced when Iran reported three more tanker attacks in Hormuz on Thursday. Iran says it will discuss its nuclear proposal only after the US meets seven demands, including lifting the naval blockade. The Brent–WTI gap is now about $7.50 on mixed contract months, down from $13.52. We again could not find an October Indonesian Crude Price print.

WTI fell about 13% to $90.83 and Brent about 13% to $98.32 as the market priced a deal. Last week oil was the one market still paying for risk. This week it paid out. The war premium is now a bet on diplomacy, and Thursday's tanker attacks show how fast it can reverse.

02 Crypto & Technology Crypto

Last week we asked whether BTC would reclaim $80k or test the $76,176 low. It did both. BTC opened the week at $78,259, closed Wednesday at $80,448 and then fell 5.5% on Thursday (UTC) to $75,993 as the Nasdaq sold off. It finished Friday at $75,760, down 1.9% on the week, with a Friday low of $74,770, below last week's $76,176. ETH closed at $2,236 (−0.3%) after a 7.7% Thursday drop from $2,423. ETH/BTC firmed to about 0.0295 from 0.0290.

Equities set the tone. CNN reported a tech selloff on Thursday that wiped $2.5 trillion from US stocks. Friday brought a partial rebound: the S&P 500 rose 0.3% to 7,376.86, the Nasdaq 1.1% to 24,579.43 and the Dow fell 0.5% to 51,285.73. For the week the Nasdaq lost 3.4%, the S&P 2.7% and the Dow 0.9%. The Nasdaq is now more than 3% below the 25,437 close we flagged last week.

October payrolls beat forecasts by a wide margin and yields edged up, with the 10-year near 4.85%; sources differ by a few basis points. Recaps put December hike odds at about 70%. That is the opposite of last week's relief. We could not confirm this week's spot Bitcoin ETF flows, so we do not report them.

BTC fell 1.9% to $75,760 and ETH 0.3% to $2,236 after a 5.5% Thursday plunge and a 3.4% Nasdaq weekly loss. Bitcoin did reclaim $80k, for one close. It could not hold it once tech broke, and a strong jobs print keeps a December hike alive.

03 Pasar Saham Indonesia IDX

Last week we asked whether the JCI would hold 5,000. On a closing basis it did not, briefly. The index fell 3.77% to 4,904 on Tuesday, then recovered to close the week at 5,050.42, down 0.13% from 5,057.05. It rose 0.40% on Friday. Market capitalisation slipped 1.34% to Rp8,744 trillion. The year-to-date fall is about 41.5% per OJK data cited by Media Keuangan.

Foreign flow turned from heavy selling to near balance. Weekly net selling was reported between Rp167 billion and about Rp1.07 trillion depending on the market scope, against about Rp4.2 trillion the week before. Foreigners were net buyers of about Rp328 billion on Friday. The 2026 total is still near Rp98 trillion. The market now waits for the MSCI November review on Wednesday, November 11, where Indonesia's standalone status is the question. MNC Sekuritas sees a range of 4,824 to 5,247 for this week. We did not confirm implementation details of the High Shareholding Concentration rule.

The rupiah snapped a four-week losing streak. Spot closed at 18,089 per dollar per Bloomberg via Kontan, 0.32% stronger on the week from 18,147. It peaked at 18,168 on Tuesday, so 18,200 was not tested. Bank Indonesia held the BI-Rate at 5.75% on October 21 and began cutting bank reserve requirements, to 8% from November 1 and 7% from December 1. The next meeting is calendared for November 17–18.

The JCI ended flat at 5,050, foreign selling faded to under Rp1.1 trillion and the rupiah firmed to 18,089. Cheaper oil helped the rupiah, and a quiet week put the index back where it started. Everything now hangs on one date: the MSCI decision on November 11.
