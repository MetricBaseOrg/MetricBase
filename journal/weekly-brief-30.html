Weekly Brief — #30 · Week of November 23, 2026

Bitcoin Falls 13.8% to $58,824, the Nasdaq Drops 10%, and a Bank Indonesia Hike Fails to Hold the Rupiah at 18,270 — What the Data Says

An AI-led selloff took the Nasdaq down 10% and the S&P 500 into a correction, and Bitcoin lost 13.8% to $58,824. Bank Indonesia hiked to 6.00% against a consensus hold, yet the rupiah closed at a record-weak 18,270 and the JCI fell 3.71% to 5,146. Brent settled at $92.57 for a third weekly loss.

01 Energy Markets Energy

Last week we asked whether Brent would hold $95. It did not. January Brent settled at $92.57 on Friday, up 59 cents on the day but down 3.4% from $95.84. December WTI settled at $83.98 in its final session, down $4.58 or 5.2% from $88.56. The January WTI contract settled at $83.41. That is a third straight weekly loss for both grades.

The IEA surplus call is playing out in the tape. CNBC reports Gulf crude shipments through Hormuz are back near pre-war levels. Thursday's WTI settle of $83.21 was the lowest since August 26, and Brent spent the week in the low $90s. The blockade has not moved, and Iran is preparing tankers to test it. Friday's bounce was tension, not a change in trend.

The Brent–WTI gap widened to $8.59 from $7.28, but that compares January Brent with expiring December WTI. On matching January contracts it is $9.16. Next up: OPEC+ meets on November 29 to set 2027 quotas, with the market already pricing a surplus.

Brent fell 3.4% to $92.57 and WTI 5.2% to $83.98 as Hormuz flows recovered. Three weeks of losses have taken about $25 off Brent from $117. The war premium is mostly gone, and OPEC+ now has to price a surplus, not a shortage.

02 Crypto & Technology Crypto

Last week we asked whether BTC would reclaim $70k or hold the $62,000 to $66,000 band. Neither held. BTC never traded above $65,667 all week, closed below $62,000 on Thursday and finished Friday at $58,824, down 13.8% from $68,258. Friday's low of $57,666 undercut the $57,803 July low that analysts had flagged. ETH fell 16.0% to $1,619, and ETH/BTC slipped to about 0.0275 from 0.0283.

Last week stocks did not follow crypto down. This week they did. An AI-led selloff sent the Nasdaq down 5.28% on Friday to 22,617.73, a 10% weekly loss and its worst week since March 2020. The S&P 500 fell 8.6% on the week to 6,910.39 and is now in a correction. The Nasdaq lost the 24,579 level we flagged and turned negative for 2026.

Rates were not the trigger this time. The 10-year fell to about 4.92% to 4.94% on Friday as money ran to Treasuries, down about 20 basis points from 5.14%. FedWatch still prices a December 9 hike at about 99%, per CNBC, and Fed Governor Waller said 50 basis points is likely needed. Spot Bitcoin ETFs lost about $1.25 billion on the week per Farside, with Friday not fully tallied. The outflow streak did not turn. The damage ran into Saturday, with a low of $55,030, before a partial rebound.

BTC fell 13.8% to $58,824 and the Nasdaq 10% while the 10-year fell about 20 basis points. Last week Bitcoin traded as the most rate-sensitive asset. This week yields fell and it still dropped harder than tech. It is trading as high-beta risk, not as a hedge.

03 Pasar Saham Indonesia IDX

Last week we asked whether Bank Indonesia would hold at 5.75% or hike. It hiked. On November 18 the board raised the BI-Rate 25 basis points to 6.00%, with the deposit facility at 5.25% and the lending facility at 6.75%. Most economists had expected a hold. BI said the move protects the rupiah and gets ahead of a Fed hike. It is keeping liquidity loose through a second 1-percentage-point reserve requirement cut on December 1.

The currency did not take the hint. Spot closed Friday at 18,270 per dollar per Kontan, a record-weak close and 0.35% weaker on the week. That breaks the 18,239 record we flagged. JISDOR fixed at 18,278. The hike bought the rupiah nothing against a global risk-off move.

Equities paid for it. The JCI fell every session, losing 1.07% on hike day and 1.02% to 5,194 on Thursday. It closed Friday at 5,146.33, down 3.71% from 5,344.41. It lost 5,250 but held 5,000. Foreigners sold a net Rp2.61 trillion on the week per IDX data, about Rp947 billion of it on Friday, which reversed last week's buying. The 2026 net sell is now Rp100.23 trillion. Average daily value fell 47.9% to Rp12.35 trillion. BRPT and BRMS fell hard ahead of their MSCI deletions.
