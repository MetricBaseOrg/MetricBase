Research Report · IDX × Rupiah × Rates · October 2026

The Standalone
Test

On 16 October MSCI proposed moving Indonesia out of the Emerging Market index family to Standalone status. The JCI lost 14.9% in a week, then regained 4.9%. The rupiah broke 18,000 and has not come back. This report sizes the flow, traces the transmission to the currency, and explains why the free float, not the index, is the real issue.

Bun Lead Analyst · MetricBase
Published 26 Oct 2026
Read time ~10 min
Data as of 23 Oct 2026
IDX Rupiah Rates
Section 01

Executive Summary

On Friday 16 October MSCI opened a consultation on moving Indonesia from Emerging Market to Standalone Market status. Nothing has been decided. Feedback is open until 30 April 2027, with a final decision expected by 30 June 2027. The market treated the proposal as a verdict anyway: the JCI fell 14.91% in five sessions, 11.57% of it on the Friday.

A week later the JCI had regained 4.90% to 5,439. Foreigners were still selling and the rupiah closed at 18,080, its weakest close in our briefs. The analyst estimates of the passive outflow are $3 billion to $6 billion, small against $146.3 billion of reserves. The harm is in what the proposal says about ownership, not in the arithmetic of one index.

The passive-flow estimates are a rounding error against reserves. What MSCI is questioning is who owns Indonesian shares, and no policy rate answers that. That is why the equity market and the rupiah are now moving on the same variable.

Section 02

What MSCI Actually Proposed

MSCI said Indonesia no longer merits Emerging Market status and proposed Standalone, a category below Frontier. It cited shareholding opacity, suspected coordinated trading and insufficient enforcement. It also froze several Indonesia index changes, including increases to foreign inclusion factors.

This did not come from nowhere. MSCI froze Indonesian index changes in February 2026 over limited ownership transparency, and said in June it could consider a Frontier consultation if progress was not evident by its November review.

The timeline matters for how to read the rest of this report. Every figure below is a reaction to a proposal. The decision is eight months away, and the exchange and regulator have until then to change the facts MSCI is judging.

The flows are not the same money, but it shows the market has been paying for the ownership concern for months, not days.

Section 04

Market capitalisation fell by about Rp1,544 trillion in the shock week to Rp9,157 trillion, then recovered to Rp9,583 trillion. That leaves it about Rp1,118 trillion below the pre-shock level, and the index 10.7% below 6,094 (MetricBase calculations from the reported figures). Year to date the JCI is down about 38%.

The bounce was domestic. Foreigners sold Rp3.34 trillion net on the Friday of the shock and Rp6.67 trillion over the following week by Katadata's count, or Rp8.72 trillion by other outlets. The 2026 total rose from Rp88.19 trillion to Rp94.87 trillion.

A rebound that foreigners did not join is liquidity, not resolution. Domestic money bought the dip; the foreign bid, which MSCI membership exists to attract, did not return.

Section 05

Why the Float Is the Real Issue

MSCI's complaint is structural. Many Indonesian companies have a small public float and concentrated, opaque ownership, which makes prices easier to move and harder to trust. Regulators and the exchange know this and have moved on three fronts.

Measure Detail What we could not confirm
Minimum free float 7.5% to 15% Announced 29 Jan; 327 issuers affected; reporting conflicts on when it binds
HSC stocks out of the JCI 80 stocks, ~18% weight Press reports say from 2 Nov; no IDX notice found
Shareholder disclosure Holdings above 1% Single-source report; start date unclear
IDX talks with MSCI 19–20 Oct No MSCI reply published

The HSC step deserves a careful reading. IDX says it is not a delisting: the stocks keep trading, but their prices stop moving the JCI. The list is reviewed every three months. It changes what the local benchmark shows. Our reading is that it does not change who owns the shares, which is what MSCI is judging.

Raising the float is the reform that would change the facts. Excluding HSC stocks from the JCI changes the headline number, and 327 issuers still need to sell down or issue. That is slow, and a thin market has to absorb it.

Section 06

The Rupiah Channel

Equity outflows are dollar demand. Bank Indonesia met on 20–21 October with the rupiah at the edge of 18,000, and held the BI-Rate at 5.75% for a fourth straight meeting. It raised the Deposit Facility rate 25 bp to 5.00%, left the Lending Facility at 6.50%, and announced phased cuts to rupiah reserve requirements from 1 November and 1 December.

The hold was not a clean surprise. Economists split: 15 of 29 in Reuters' poll expected no change, and about half of Bloomberg's 38. Sources disagree on the new reserve ratios, so we do not quote them.

BI says its hedging incentives (swaps and domestic non-deliverable forwards) have brought in about $8 billion to $9 billion, and a 20 October report puts the cumulative figure at $9.9 billion. The rupiah has still fallen about 7.9% this year. MUFG's 19 October forecast is 18,350 for Q4 2026 and 18,500 for Q1 2027, with a depreciation bias.

BI is running a policy mix: hold the rate, lift the floor of the corridor, add liquidity, subsidise hedging. That manages the pace of the rupiah's fall. It does not give a foreign fund a reason to own Indonesian equities that MSCI says it cannot see into.

Section 07

The Backdrop MSCI Landed On

The proposal arrived into a week when every external gauge was already stressed. Oil kept rising, US yields sat near their highest since 2002, and risk assets were holding on rates relief rather than demand.

We could not find an October read on Indonesia's 10-year government bond yield. The last figures we found, about 7% on 24 September against a 5.195% US 10-year, are too old to use for a spread. This is a gap in this report.

Oil at $116 widens the current account deficit that the rupiah already carries, and the US 10-year at 5% raises the return foreign money can earn elsewhere. MSCI did not create the squeeze on Indonesian assets. It removed the argument that the equity market was a reason to stay.

Section 08

What to Watch

These are structural markers, not predictions or investment recommendations.

The ownership data

Whether the 15% float requirement gets a dated calendar for the 327 affected issuers, and whether 1% ownership disclosure is live. These are the facts MSCI is judging.

Foreign flow on up days

The JCI rose on Friday with foreigners selling Rp1.28 trillion. A rally with a foreign net buy would be the first real sign that the market treats the proposal as reversible.

Fed and rupiah levels

The 27–28 October Fed decision, then the rupiah against 18,100 and MUFG's 18,350 Q4 forecast. A December US hike narrows BI's 175 bp cushion unless BI moves.

Calendar

The 1 November reserve requirement cut, the reported 2 November HSC exclusion, MSCI's November index review, and the consultation close on 30 April 2027. Our editorial this week argues that Indonesia is currently answering the wrong question.

Read the ownership data, not the index level. The JCI can recover without the reform, and the rupiah will not.
