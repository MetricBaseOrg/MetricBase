Clarification
Is Not a Reform

MSCI said Indonesia has an ownership problem. The exchange asked MSCI to explain itself, took 80 concentrated stocks out of the JCI, and watched the index bounce 4.9% while foreigners kept selling. A proposal is not a verdict, but the response so far is not an answer either.

Bun Lead Analyst · MetricBase
Published 26 Oct 2026
Read time ~6 min
Type Opinion
Opinion IDX Rupiah

On 16 October MSCI told the market what it thinks of Indonesian share ownership: opaque, open to coordinated trading, and not enforced hard enough. It proposed Standalone status, below Frontier. The JCI fell 11.57% that day. Three days later the exchange met MSCI online, and the next day it asked MSCI for clarification of its reasoning.

We think that is the wrong first move. MSCI did not write in code. It named three problems. The job is to fix them, not to ask what they mean.

What the response actually changes

The headline measure is the decision to exclude High Shareholding Concentration stocks from the JCI calculation. That covers 80 stocks, about 18% of the index weight, per press reports. IDX says it is not a delisting. The shares keep trading. They just stop moving the benchmark.

That is a fair clean-up of the local index. It is also a change to a number Indonesians read, not a change to who owns the shares. Our reading is that MSCI judges ownership, not how the exchange weights its own composite. The 18% of the JCI that is concentrated is still concentrated tomorrow.

The second measure, a 15% minimum free float, is the one that bites. It was announced on 29 January, when 327 issuers were named as affected. Nearly nine months later MSCI opened a consultation anyway. Reporting conflicts on when the rule actually binds, which is itself the point: an announced rule that markets cannot date does not move a classification committee.

MSCI listed ownership opacity, suspected coordinated trading, and weak enforcement. A request for clarification addresses none of the three, and an index recalculation addresses only the first, in the wrong place.

The bounce is a tell

The JCI regained 4.90% last week, to 5,439, with all 11 sectors higher on Friday and the big banks leading. Foreigners sold Rp1.28 trillion that same Friday and between Rp6.67 trillion and Rp8.72 trillion over the week. Their 2026 total is Rp94.87 trillion, about $5.2 billion at 18,080 (MetricBase calculation).

Domestic money treated a proposal as a pardon. It may turn out to be right. But MSCI membership exists to attract the money that did not come back, and the index is still about Rp1,100 trillion short of its pre-shock capitalisation (MetricBase calculation).

The rupiah did not take the pardon. It closed at 18,080 on Friday, its weakest close in these briefs, a week after Bank Indonesia held at 5.75%, lifted the Deposit Facility rate to 5.00% and said it would start cutting reserve requirements on 1 November. BI says its hedging incentives have drawn about $9.9 billion since June. Currency and equities are now telling the same story.

The other side of the argument

The defence of the current approach has substance. IDX reached MSCI within three days. The HSC exclusion is a real step. The consultation runs to 30 April 2027, and a decision is not due until 30 June, so there is time to show results. A request for clarification is also how an exchange gets MSCI to put its criteria in writing.

There is a market-structure case for going slowly. Forcing 327 issuers to lift their free float at once means controlling holders selling into a thin market, and a higher float does not automatically bring liquidity, since institutions stay selective and retail capacity to absorb shares is limited. And the passive money is not decisive: estimates of $3 billion to $6 billion are roughly 2% to 4% of the $146.3 billion in reserves (MetricBase calculation).

We accept all of that. It argues for a sensible schedule. It does not argue for a schedule MSCI cannot see. Slow is defensible when it is dated and published. Slow and vague is what the market has worried about since the February MSCI freeze.

What follows

Judge the next six months by three tests, not by the index level.

First, a dated free float calendar for the 327 issuers, with named milestones. Second, 1% ownership disclosure live and the concentrated-ownership list published, not announced. Third, foreign flow on up days. A JCI rally with Rp1 trillion of daily foreign selling is liquidity, not resolution.

The calendar is short. The Fed decides on 28 October with a December hike priced near 75% as of 21 October. BI starts cutting reserve requirements on 1 November. The HSC exclusion is reported for 2 November, though we found no exchange notice confirming the date. Our research report this week sizes the flows and the rupiah channel in full.

"An exchange can ask what a classification means. It cannot ask a classification committee to look away. The only answer MSCI has asked for is a market where you can see who owns what."

— MetricBase editorial position
